Both at the same build stage (gap-filled + polished). v2 used a PacBio+Hi-C reference (GCF_904066995.2) to scaffold against, vs v1's 2014 short-read reference (GCF_000633615.1) — the underlying Colombian reads and assembly method (SPAdes → RagTag → TGS-GapCloser → NextPolish) are otherwise the same.

Reading the genome fraction gap v2 wins on essentially every metric that measures assembly quality — far fewer misassemblies, better long-range accuracy (NA50), less duplication, more complete gene content, and 25× fewer contigs. Genome fraction is the one exception, and it's a reference-composition effect, not a real regression: v2's reference (Hi-C-scaffolded) successfully placed nearly all of its sequence onto its 24 main chromosomes, leaving only a small (10.2Mb) "unplaced" leftover — but that leftover is disproportionately the genuinely hardest, most repetitive material, which this short-read-based Colombian assembly can't reach either way. v1's reference, being a fragmented 2014 short-read assembly, has thousands of small unplaced fragments (34.9Mb total) that are just ordinary sequence a scaffolding algorithm couldn't connect — not hard to align to, so v1's apparent completeness is partly inflated by a reference that never demanded coverage of its hardest regions in the first place. Confirmed directly: coverage of the 24 main chromosomes alone is comparable between versions (v1: 84.9%, v2: 86.6% — v2 slightly ahead), while coverage of each version's residual unplaced scaffolds diverges sharply (v1: ~60%, v2: ~6%).

Built with bcftools consensus -H A over the 3 Controls' PASS variants at ≥66.7% frequency among them (2/3 majority), applied to the Guanapo/Trinidad reference. Includes a .chain file for exact coordinate liftover — needed because the indel-driven cumulative drift is not negligible (up to ~2,800bp at the bdnf locus, ~5,300bp across the whole chromosome).
