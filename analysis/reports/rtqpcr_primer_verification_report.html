Do the Lab's RT-qPCR Primers Still Target the Right mRNA?

Five RT-qPCR primer pairs already in routine use in the lab — bdnf, beta-actin, rpl13a (original + a SNP-free replacement), and myosin — checked against both reference genome assemblies and the Colombian pseudogenome with codes/analysis/verify_rtqpcr_primers.py: located by BLAST, confirmed against a real annotated gene (not a symbol guess), realigned to the spliced mRNA with an indel-aware aligner, and cross-checked for population variants at the binding site. Also includes 3 newly designed housekeeping-gene candidates (a pan-paralog myosin marker, gapdh, ef1a) — see New Candidates.

Functional for mRNA amplification regardless of which of the three genomes the template effectively derives from.

↻ miosina_guppy_F/R match a real tandem myosin gene family — not a perfect single-transcript hit

Guppy's fast-skeletal-muscle myosin heavy chain genes are annotated under generic LOC######## IDs with no short gene symbol, sitting in a tandem cluster of paralogs on the same chromosome in both assemblies. Both primers resolve to real, annotated myosin heavy chain genes at 90–100% identity — miosina_guppy_F to LOC103468881 (v1) / LOC108166486 (v2), both 95%; miosina_guppy_R to LOC108166486 (v1, 100%) / LOC103468749 (v2, 90%) — via an indel-aware alignment against each candidate's spliced mRNA, not a plain same-length comparison (which would misrepresent a clean 1bp indel as several unrelated substitutions).

Landing on a slightly different paralog per primer/genome-version is expected for a tandemly duplicated gene family this recently diverged — not evidence of a wrong design. Still worth an empirical check (melt curve, gel band size, or amplicon sequencing) to confirm which paralog(s) actually amplify, since more than one may cross-react if that's acceptable for a general fast-twitch-muscle marker.

Colombian pseudogenome check (added 2026-09-11): both primers are IDENTICAL against the pseudogenome — no additional Colombian-population variant on top of the paralog differences already described above. This check is necessarily partial: since neither primer has a single exact whole-length genomic match, only the exact-matched core BLAST recovers (15/19bp for the F primer, 16/20bp for R) could be lifted over and compared — not the full primer footprint. Within that core, though, v1 and the pseudogenome agree exactly, so whatever is already known about these primers (the paralog-level mismatches above) is what limits them, not a population-specific issue on top of it.

New Candidates: Housekeeping Primers

The lab uses myosin as a reference/housekeeping gene against which to contrast bdnf expression after CRISPR KO. For that role, a gene needs stable, reproducible amplification across all treatment groups — not a match to one specific transcript — so these candidates use a different design strategy than the sections above: myosin_conserved deliberately targets a region shared by the whole tandem paralog family (not one paralog), while gapdh_new and ef1a_new add two standard, single-copy reference genes as alternatives/complements. None of the three are in lab use yet — proposed here, verified in silico, pending empirical validation (melt curve / gel / qPCR efficiency).

✓ myosin_conserved: one primer pair, at least 6 tandem paralog copies, zero off-target elsewhere

Design method: aligned the spliced mRNA of the 4 annotated myosin heavy chain paralogs in the tandem cluster (LOC108166486, LOC108166487, LOC103468749, LOC145552582) with muscle, and found a 470bp block with only 2 isolated single-base differences and no indels across all 4 — a genuinely shared region, not a guess. eprimer3 then designed primers inside it, each screened directly against every paralog's own spliced mRNA to confirm an exact match (not just alignment-implied).

primersearch against the whole v2 genome confirmed the design goal empirically: the same 198bp product forms at 6 distinct genomic positions across the tandem cluster (one more paralog than originally targeted — LOC103468880, previously annotated only as "uncharacterized", turns out to carry the same conserved motif), and nowhere else in the genome. In v1, whose annotation of this locus is known to be fragmentary (see the myosin finding above), the same clean 198bp product still forms at one locus, confirming the sequence itself — not just v2's better-annotated gene model — is genuinely conserved.

Both primers fall within a single exon (like BDNF_F/R and Beta_actin_F already in the panel), so genomic DNA contamination could in principle amplify the same 198bp size — standard DNase treatment during RNA extraction is the usual mitigation, not a flaw specific to this design.

✓ gapdh_new and ef1a_new: standard single-copy references, both clean

gapdh and eef1a1a (the canonical "a" paralog — not eef1a1l2/l3, nor the spermatogenic-specific gapdhs paralog) are single-copy, orthologous (identical GeneID) in v1 and v2, avoiding the tandem-duplication ambiguity that motivated the conserved-region approach for myosin. gapdh_new_R additionally spans a real exon-exon junction (like Beta_actin_R/rpl_13a_R) — it cannot amplify genomic DNA at all, the strongest specificity guarantee available.

Method

Verifying an existing RT-qPCR primer is a different problem from designing a new CRISPR on-/off-target primer (see the Primer Design Report's Methods): the target is spliced mRNA, not genomic DNA, and the gene is unknown up front rather than chosen before design. codes/analysis/verify_rtqpcr_primers.py runs this checklist end to end:

  1. 1
    Locate by BLAST (blastn-short) against the whole genome — not a hand-picked gene list. A gene-symbol regex silently misses guppy's un-symbolled LOC######## myosin genes; BLAST doesn't have that blind spot.
  2. 2
    Annotate by spatial GFF overlap at the hit position, regardless of gene symbol — resolves the same LOC-gene blind spot from the annotation side.
  3. 3
    Realign with EMBOSS water (indel-aware, both orientations), never a fixed-length mismatch count — a single 1bp indel can otherwise look like several unrelated substitutions.
  4. 4
    Cross-check both genome versions independently — a gene's transcript model can be complete in one assembly and fragmentary in the other for the identical GeneID.
  5. 5
    Population check via exact liftover (.chain + CrossMap) of the matched genomic footprint to the Colombian pseudogenome, for exact single-exon v1 hits — a primer matching genomic DNA is not automatically an mRNA primer.

python3 codes/analysis/verify_rtqpcr_primers.py --primers-csv pairs.csv --ref-versions v1,v2 --population-check — full usage in TUTORIAL.md §4, rationale for each step (with the real mis-identifications each one catches) in PIPELINE.md §10. Raw output: analysis/rtqpcr_verification/rtqpcr_primer_verification.csv.

The 3 new candidates (above) were designed, not just verified: muscle (multiple sequence alignment) found the myosin conserved region, eprimer3 (EMBOSS + primer3_core — same tool as the Primer Design Report) generated candidates within it, primersearch confirmed real in-silico PCR products genome-wide, and every candidate was then run back through the same verify_rtqpcr_primers.py checklist above before being listed here — the same battery of tools used throughout this project, applied in the opposite direction (proposing a sequence, rather than locating one already in use).
