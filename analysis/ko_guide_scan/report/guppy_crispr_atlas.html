Criteria for Choosing the Best Guide

The optimal position within the gene differs by system: CRISPRko aims to break the protein by editing the DNA; CRISPRi aims to silence transcription without cutting. Your intuition is fundamentally right, with important nuances at both ends.

CRISPRko Cas9 · double-strand cut

Goal: NHEJ indel → frameshift → truncated/degraded protein (NMD)

  1. Early, constitutive exon. Ideally in the first third of the CDS, present in all relevant isoforms — a frameshift there drags down the rest of the protein. Avoid the last exon: a premature stop codon there often escapes NMD (nonsense-mediated decay) and can produce a partially functional truncated protein instead of a real null.
  2. No population variants in the PAM or seed. Use only IDENTICAL guides against the pseudogenome — a broken PAM or a SNP in the 10bp proximal to the PAM can abolish cutting in the population you're actually going to edit.
  3. High specificity, low real off-target. High CRISPOR mitSpecScore (>90) and low offtargetCount — remember Cas-OFFinder/CRISPOR search the whole genome, not just the CDS.
  4. Reasonable cutting efficiency. A higher Doench 2016 score = higher probability of an indel per edited allele, but it's the weakest signal of the four — don't sacrifice position or specificity for a few Doench points.
  5. Avoid exon-exon junctions. A candidate that only exists in the spliced CDS (not in contiguous genomic DNA) isn't editable — already filtered as JUNCTION in this table.

CRISPRi dCas9-KRAB · no cutting

Goal: block RNA polymerase recruitment/progression near the TSS

  1. Narrow window around the TSS. The literature (Gilbert et al. 2014; Horlbeck et al. 2016) converges on −50 to +300 bp relative to the transcription start site — not the "promoter" in general. Outside that window, silencing drops sharply no matter how good the guide is.
  2. Strand matters less than position. Contrary to common assumption, dCas9-KRAB works on both strands within the window — prioritize distance to the TSS, not orientation.
  3. Never inside the CDS. CRISPRi guides downstream of the TSS (in internal exons) barely silence — RNAPII blocking is only efficient near the start.
  4. Also avoid population variants in that window. The same "no SNP in PAM/seed" criterion applies — but here the window of interest is genomic (TSS ±350bp), not the CDS we already scanned.
  5. Specificity over efficiency. Since there's no repair involved, a real off-target is costlier (off-target silencing) — prioritize mitSpecScore/offtargetCount even more than for KO.

Methods and Limitations

The guides shown and their metrics/off-targets come from crispor.py itself (Singularity container) run against the registered reference genome: CRISPRko with the representative transcript's full CDS as input, CRISPRi with the −50/+300bp genomic window around its TSS (mRNA start, not the CDS start). Population-safety classification (IDENTICAL/PAM_BROKEN/SEED_VARIANT/DISTAL_VARIANT) still comes from the project's own alignment (minimap2 --cs) against the same region in the Colombian pseudogenome — ko_guide_scan.py / crispri_tss_scan.py — and is joined to each CRISPOR guide by its exact 23bp sequence (spacer+PAM). Only IDENTICAL candidates are shown; real off-targets (top 3 per guide, ordered by mismatches↑/CFD↓) come from the off-target TSV that crispor.py already computes against the whole genome.

⚠ CRISPRko — agap3, grin1a, and gria1a (v1 tab only) have no CRISPOR scores: the v1 reference genome (2014 short-read assembly) contains ambiguous IUPAC codes (R/Y/S/W/K/M) in the CDS of those regions — likely unresolved heterozygous sites. Confirmed this is a hard, unhandled failure inside crispor.py itself, at two different points in its code that don't tolerate characters outside A/C/G/T/N: for agap3 and grin1a, its revComp() function (used when extracting the flanking sequence to count off-targets) doesn't recognize the letter 'S' (KeyError: 'S'); for gria1a, the Azimuth-2.0 efficiency model (Doench'16) doesn't recognize the letter 'R' in its fixed encoding alphabet (ValueError: 'R' is not in list). The manual scan and variant classification are unaffected; only the additional specificity/efficiency ranking is missing for those 3 genes under v1. CRISPRi is unaffected for all 8 genes under v1 (including agap3, which originally hit the same problem here too) — its TSS window is a different, much smaller genomic region than the CDS, so it usually avoids an ambiguity code even when the CDS doesn't; agap3 specifically stopped hitting it once its CRISPRi representative transcript changed to a different promoter 63.8kb away (see the TSS coverage note below) — a side effect of that fix, not a deliberate one.
✓ Resolved under v2 (2026-09-17): the newer male reference genome (GCF_904066995.2, PacBio+Hi-C, 2025) has no ambiguity codes at these genes' guide windows — all 3 genes now have full CRISPOR coverage (559/559 agap3, 442/442 grin1a, 387/387 gria1a guide-comparison rows scored). Switch to the v2 · Male PacBio+Hi-C tab above to see these genes' CRISPOR-ranked candidates.

Isoforms column (added 2026-09-14): ko_guide_scan.py designs against one representative isoform (the longest CDS shared with the pseudogenome) — a candidate can be a perfect IDENTICAL match to the population there while still sitting in a region absent from the gene's OTHER isoforms, so an edit might not disrupt every protein the gene actually produces. The Isoforms column shows how many of the gene's annotated isoforms (reference genome) share that exact genomic footprint in their own CDS — 7/7 means every isoform is hit; a lower count is flagged amber. This is now the first ranking criterion for CRISPRko (above last-exon avoidance and CRISPOR scores): most genes here have a solidly constitutive core (80-96% of candidates), but agap3 is a sharp exception — only 105/557 (19%) of its candidates are constitutive across its 7 very differently-structured isoforms (2412-3966bp CDS, 9-18 exons) — so this reranking specifically matters for agap3's guide choice.

CRISPRi TSS coverage + representative choice (added 2026-09-14): the analogous isoform question for CRISPRi isn't "is this CDS position shared" but "do all isoforms even start transcription at the same place" — checked by comparing every annotated isoform's own transcription start site (TSS) against the chosen 350bp window. Most genes cluster tightly (a single window covers all isoforms trivially), but bdnf (6 distinct TSS, 3.8kb spread) and agap3 (6 distinct TSS, a striking 63.8kb spread) have genuine, independently RNA-seq-supported alternative promoters — every isoform's model has "100% coverage by RNAseq alignments" with 5-44 supporting samples, not a weak or fragmentary prediction; bdnf's pattern also matches this gene's well-documented multi-promoter architecture in other vertebrates (Aid et al. 2007). Because CDS length says nothing about promoter usage, the CRISPRi representative transcript is now chosen independently of the CRISPRko one, by strongest RNA-seq support for its own TSS — this changed the pick for agap3 specifically: its longest-CDS transcript (used for CRISPRko) sits in a tight cluster with 13-22 samples of support each, but a transcript 63.8kb further upstream has 44 samples, the strongest of all 7 - it is now the CRISPRi representative, and the report explicitly flags that this window does not reach the other cluster at all.
